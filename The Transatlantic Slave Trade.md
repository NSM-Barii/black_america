# The Transatlantic Slave Trade

---

## First — Context Most People Skip

Slavery existed in Africa before Europeans arrived. That is a fact. African kingdoms enslaved prisoners of war, debtors, and criminals. This was common across the ancient world — it existed in Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and Africa. It was brutal but it operated on a certain scale and within certain rules. Enslaved people in many African societies could:
- Earn their freedom
- Marry
- Own property
- Their children were not automatically enslaved

That is a fundamentally different system than what came next.

---

## What Europeans Built

When Portuguese explorers began trading along the West African coast in the 1400s they encountered existing slave trade networks. They didn't invent slavery in Africa — they plugged into it, then transformed it into something unrecognizable.

What they built was **chattel slavery** — meaning enslaved people were legally classified as property. Not prisoners of war. Not debtors. Property. Like a chair or a horse. And critically — that status passed to your children automatically. You were born property. Your children were born property.

This had never existed at that scale anywhere in human history.

---

## The Numbers

- Approximately **12.5 million Africans** were loaded onto slave ships between 1500 and 1900
- Roughly **1.8 million died** on the Middle Passage alone — the ocean crossing — and were thrown overboard
- Millions more died in the process of capture and in holding forts before boarding
- Some historians estimate the total loss to Africa — deaths, people removed, population growth that never happened — at **100 million people**

100 million people removed from a continent over 400 years.

---

## The Triangle Trade

It operated in three legs:

1. European ships left Europe carrying manufactured goods — guns, cloth, alcohol
2. Those goods were traded to African kingdoms and intermediaries in exchange for enslaved people
3. Enslaved Africans were shipped to the Americas and Caribbean
4. Ships returned to Europe loaded with sugar, cotton, tobacco — products produced by enslaved labor

Every leg was profitable. The entire early economy of the Western world was built on this system.

---

## African Complicity — The Uncomfortable Truth

Some African kingdoms actively participated in capturing and selling other Africans to European traders. The Kingdom of Dahomey in modern day Benin was one of the most aggressive — raiding neighboring kingdoms specifically to sell captives to European traders. They grew wealthy from it.

This is important to understand clearly:

- It does not reduce European responsibility — Europeans created the demand, the system, and the ideology that justified it
- It does not mean Africans were selling "their own people" in the way we think of it — ethnic and national identity worked differently then. A Dahomean did not see an Igbo prisoner as a brother any more than an English soldier saw a French prisoner as a brother
- But it does mean the story is complex and anyone who gives you a simple version is selling you something

---

## What It Destroyed

The slave trade didn't just remove bodies from Africa. It deliberately and systematically destroyed:

- **Identity** — enslaved people were stripped of names, languages, religions, and family connections
- **History** — generations grew up knowing nothing of the civilizations we just studied
- **Psychology** — centuries of being told you are subhuman, property, inferior creates damage that doesn't disappear when slavery legally ends

This is the part that connects directly to [[Malcolm X]]. When he talked about black people needing to reclaim their identity — this is what he was responding to. Not just slavery itself but the 400 year project of making black people forget who they were before it.
