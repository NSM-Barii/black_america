# The Central Question — Build Within or Outside the System?

This is the unresolved question at the heart of black political thought for over 400 years. It is not settled. Every major thinker in this tradition has wrestled with it. This note tracks the debate and my developing position on it.

---

## The Debate

**[[Marcus Garvey]] — Build Outside**
Separation and self-determination. Black people building their own nation, economy, and institutions completely independent of white American systems. You cannot be free inside a system built on your subjugation.

**[[W.E.B. Du Bois]] — Work Within**
Black people are American. They have earned full citizenship through centuries of labor and sacrifice. The goal is full integration and equal rights achieved through political organizing, legal challenges, and intellectual excellence within existing institutions.

This debate is still happening today — every time someone says "support black owned businesses" vs "we need better policies and legislation" you are hearing Garvey vs Du Bois without the names attached.

---

## The Problem With Pure Separation

**Black Wall Street — Greenwood, Tulsa 1921**
The most successful example of a self-sufficient black economy in American history. Built entirely outside the white economic system. In 1921 a white mob burned it to the ground with the support of local government and police. The system reached in and destroyed it anyway.

**The core problem:** Every piece of land in America is under American law, taxation, and jurisdiction. Where do you build that the system cannot touch?

---

## My Current Position

Use the system as a means to an end — not the destination. Specifically:

1. Use political organizing to gain control or heavy influence over a specific state or region
2. Within that political stronghold, sanction and protect projects centered around black economic and cultural nationalism
3. Use that political power as a shield while building black institutions, businesses, and infrastructure
4. Build from blacks for blacks under political protection

**The logic:** You need political power as a shield for economic and cultural building. The goal is still self-determination — the system is just the tool used to create protected space to get there.

---

## The Challenges to This Position

**Challenge 1 — The Concentration Question**
Black Americans are spread across the country. The most concentrated populations are in cities not states. Cities are controlled by states which are controlled by federal law. Even dominant city-level political power can be overridden, defunded, or gerrymandered away.

**Challenge 2 — The Reconstruction Precedent**
After the Civil War black people did exactly this — used political power as protection for black economic and political development. Between 1865 and 1877 it was working. Then the federal government withdrew troops as part of a political deal and allowed white supremacist militias to violently overthrow every black led government in the South. Legally. Systematically. The shield was removed the moment it became politically inconvenient.

**Challenge 3 — The Coalition Problem**
Black Americans are 13% of the national population. No state has a black majority. Gaining political control requires coalitions with other groups — which means competing interests and compromises. How do you maintain a black nationalist agenda while building the multiracial coalition you need to gain the power to pursue it?

---

## The Unanswered Question

How do you build something the system genuinely cannot touch?

Proposed answers from various thinkers:
- Sovereign land in Africa — [[Pan-Africanism]]
- Decentralized finance and cryptocurrency
- It is impossible without a fully sovereign nation state

**This question is still open. Return to it as you learn more.**
