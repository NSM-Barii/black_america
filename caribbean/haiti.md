# Haiti — The Full Story

> *"In overthrowing me, you have done no more than cut down the trunk of the tree of black liberty in Saint-Domingue. It will spring back from the roots, for they are numerous and deep."* — Toussaint L'Ouverture

---

## What Saint-Domingue Was

Saint-Domingue was the French colonial name for what became Haiti. At its peak it was the most profitable colony in the entire world. It produced:
- 40% of Europe's sugar
- 50% of Europe's coffee
- Significant cotton and indigo exports

One third of the entire Atlantic slave trade fed into Saint-Domingue. Conditions were so brutal the average enslaved person died within 7 years of arrival. France just imported more.

**Population at the time of the revolution:**
- 500,000 enslaved Africans
- 32,000 white colonists
- 28,000 free people of color

---

## The Man Before Toussaint — Dutty Boukman

Most people only know Toussaint L'Ouverture. The revolution was ignited by **Dutty Boukman.**

Boukman was an enslaved man from Jamaica, born in West Africa. He was a Houngan — a Vodou priest — and a coachman on a plantation known for his enormous physical size and commanding presence.

On the night of **August 14, 1791** Boukman led a ceremony in the forest of Bois Caïman — a Vodou ritual that became the spiritual declaration of war. Enslaved people from across the region gathered. Boukman delivered what became one of the most powerful declarations of resistance in history:

> *"Listen to the voice of liberty that speaks in the hearts of all of us."*

Eight days later on **August 22, 1791** the rebellion erupted. Within weeks over 1,000 plantations in the northern province were in flames. The revolution had begun.

Boukman was captured and killed by French forces in November 1791. The French publicly displayed his severed head to demoralize the rebels and prove he had no supernatural protection.

It didn't work.

---

## Toussaint L'Ouverture

After Boukman's death **Toussaint L'Ouverture** emerged as the dominant military and political leader.

Toussaint had been born enslaved but given unusual access to books by his enslaver. He educated himself extensively — reading Caesar's military campaigns and applying those tactics to guerrilla warfare in the Haitian mountains.

He defeated:
- The French colonial army
- A British invasion of 60,000 soldiers — the largest British military expedition in history up to that point
- A Spanish invasion

Napoleon sent his brother-in-law Charles Leclerc with 40,000 of his best soldiers to retake Saint-Domingue. Toussaint negotiated a peace agreement in 1801. Napoleon betrayed it, arrested Toussaint, and transported him to Fort de Joux prison in the French Alps where he died of cold and pneumonia in **April 1803.**

His last words: *"In overthrowing me, you have done no more than cut down the trunk of the tree of black liberty in Saint-Domingue. It will spring back from the roots, for they are numerous and deep."*

He was right.

---

## Jean-Jacques Dessalines — The Man Who Finished It

After Toussaint's capture **Jean-Jacques Dessalines** took command and delivered the final defeat of Napoleon's forces.

On **January 1, 1804** Dessalines declared independence and renamed the country **Haiti** — from the indigenous Taíno word *Ayiti* meaning "land of high mountains." A deliberate rejection of the French colonial name and a connection to the original people of the land.

Haiti became the first black republic in the history of the world. The first nation founded by formerly enslaved people.

---

## The Debt — France's Revenge

In 1825 France sent warships to Haiti with an ultimatum — pay **150 million francs** in reparations or face re-invasion and re-enslavement.

France demanded that Haiti pay reparations **to France.** Haiti was billed for its own freedom — compensating France for the loss of its enslaved people and its colony.

150 million francs = approximately **$21 billion in today's money.**

Haiti had no choice. No country would support them. They agreed to pay.

To make the first payment Haiti took out loans from French banks at crushing interest rates.

Haiti did not finish paying off this debt until **1947.** 122 years after it was imposed. Generations of Haitian wealth went to France and French banks instead of building the country.

A 2022 New York Times investigation documented that this debt cost Haiti between **$21 billion and $115 billion** in lost economic development.

This is why Haiti is the poorest country in the Western Hemisphere. Not because Haitians are incapable. Because France spent 122 years extracting the wealth of a nation as punishment for winning their freedom.

---

## The US Role

- **1804** — The US refused to recognize Haiti's independence. A successful black slave revolution 500 miles from American shores was too dangerous while America still held 1 million enslaved people.
- **1915–1934** — The United States **occupied Haiti militarily for 19 years.** American forces controlled Haiti's finances, customs, and government. When they left they had restructured Haiti's economy to benefit American banks.
- The US has intervened in Haitian politics repeatedly throughout the 20th and 21st century — supporting dictators, destabilizing governments, and maintaining economic control.

---

## The Pattern

Haiti won its freedom through the most extraordinary military achievement in the history of enslaved people. Then the world spent 200 years making it pay for that victory.

The poverty of Haiti today is not an accident. It is the direct result of deliberate, sustained economic punishment by France, the United States, and the broader global system — specifically because Haiti had the audacity to win.
