# Jamaica — The Full Story

---

## What Jamaica Was

Jamaica is a small island — about the size of Connecticut — but its impact on the history of slavery, resistance, and black culture is disproportionate to its size.

The island was originally inhabited by the **Taíno people** — the same indigenous group whose word *Ayiti* gave Haiti its name. The Taíno called the island **Xaymaca** meaning "Land of Wood and Water."

Spain colonized Jamaica in 1494. Within 50 years the Taíno population was completely wiped out through disease, slavery, and massacre. An entire people erased in half a century.

The Spanish brought the first enslaved Africans to Jamaica in the early 1500s to replace the Taíno labor they had destroyed.

---

## The British Takeover — 1655

In 1655 Britain seized Jamaica from Spain. When the British arrived the Spanish freed their enslaved Africans and armed them to fight the British.

Those freed Africans retreated into the mountainous interior rather than submit to the new colonizers. They became known as the **Maroons** — from the Spanish word *cimarrón* meaning "wild" or "untamed."

---

## The Maroons — The First Free Black Nation in the Americas

The Maroons established free communities in the mountains of Jamaica:
- **Accompong** in the west
- **Nanny Town** in the east

They built their own society, governance, and military — completely independent of British colonial control.

For nearly 80 years the Maroons fought the British in the **First Maroon War (1728–1739).** The British sent army after army into the mountains. The Maroons used guerrilla warfare — ambushes, traps, knowledge of the terrain — to defeat every expedition.

The British could not win. In **1739** they were forced to sign a **peace treaty** legally recognizing Maroon freedom and territory.

This was **126 years before American slavery ended.**

---

## Queen Nanny

Nanny was an **Asante woman from West Africa** — the Asante were one of the most powerful warrior kingdoms in Africa. She was a military strategist, spiritual leader, and commander of the eastern Maroon forces at Nanny Town.

She is credited with freeing over **1,000 enslaved people** from British plantations and leading them into the mountains. Her tactical brilliance made Nanny Town virtually impregnable.

Like [[Dutty Boukman]] in Haiti, Nanny was a practitioner of African spiritual tradition — Obeah. The British feared her spiritual power as much as her military skill.

Today Nanny is the only female national hero of Jamaica. Her face is on the Jamaican $500 bill.

---

## Jamaica's Plantation Economy

After the Maroon treaty the British consolidated their plantation system across the rest of the island. Jamaica became Britain's most valuable colony — the equivalent of what Saint-Domingue was to France.

Jamaica produced:
- The majority of Britain's sugar
- Significant coffee and rum exports

Jamaican planters were known for extreme violence — they justified it by claiming Jamaican enslaved people were more "rebellious" and needed harsher control.

They were right that Jamaican enslaved people were rebellious. They just misunderstood why — it wasn't a character flaw. It was a culture of resistance that ran deep.

---

## The Baptist War — 1831

Also known as the **Christmas Rebellion** — the largest slave rebellion in British Caribbean history.

Led by **Samuel Sharpe** — an enslaved Baptist deacon who taught himself to read and used access to newspapers to understand that the abolitionist movement in Britain was gaining momentum.

He organized a planned work stoppage — not initially a violent revolt but a **strike** demanding freedom and wages. It quickly escalated.

- Over **60,000 enslaved people** participated across the island
- Plantations burned
- The British military suppressed it brutally
- Over **500 enslaved people** were killed during and after
- Samuel Sharpe was hanged

**The critical point:** The Baptist War directly accelerated the abolition of slavery in the British Empire. The scale of the rebellion and the brutal suppression caused such outcry in Britain that Parliament passed the **Slavery Abolition Act in 1833** — just two years later.

Samuel Sharpe's rebellion ended British Caribbean slavery. He is a Jamaican national hero.

---

## Emancipation — 1834 and the Apprenticeship Scam

Slavery was officially abolished in the British Empire in 1834. But there was a catch — there is always a catch.

Formerly enslaved people were required to serve a period of **"apprenticeship"** — working for their former enslavers for free for up to **6 more years** before full freedom. It was renamed labor. It was still slavery.

Full freedom came in **1838.**

And like [[Haiti]] — Britain compensated the **slave owners** for their "loss of property":
- **£20 million** paid to enslavers across the British Caribbean
- Equivalent to **£17 billion today**
- Britain only finished paying off the loans taken to fund this compensation in **2015**

The enslaved people received nothing.

---

## Jamaica's Legacy

Jamaica punches far above its weight in global black culture and resistance:

- **[[Marcus Garvey]]** was Jamaican — the father of Pan-Africanism and black nationalism was born in St. Ann's Bay, Jamaica
- **Rastafarianism** — a spiritual and political movement rooted in African identity and the return to Africa — originated in Jamaica in the 1930s directly inspired by Garvey's ideas
- **Reggae music** — Bob Marley carried the message of black liberation and resistance to a global audience
- The **Maroon communities** still exist today — Accompong Town is still governed under the 1739 treaty

---

## The Pattern — Again

Jamaica's story follows the same pattern seen everywhere:

- Indigenous people wiped out
- Enslaved Africans imported to replace them
- People resist consistently and creatively — Maroons, Baptist War, everyday sabotage
- When freedom came it came with a bill — for the enslavers, not the enslaved
- The economic consequences of that structure persist today

Jamaica today has significant poverty, crime, and inequality. The history answers why — not Jamaican people's failings, but a system specifically designed to extract wealth and never repair the damage.
