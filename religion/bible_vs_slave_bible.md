# The Bible vs. The Slave Bible — A Direct Comparison

In 1807 British missionaries produced *Select Parts of the Holy Bible for the Use of the Negro Slaves in the British West-India Islands*. It is commonly called the Slave Bible.

It was not a summary. It was not a simplified version. It was a surgical removal of every passage that could inspire enslaved people to seek freedom — and a deliberate preservation of every passage that commanded obedience.

90% of the Old Testament was removed. 50% of the New Testament was removed.

What follows is a direct comparison of what was cut and what was kept — and why each decision was made.

---

## What Was Removed — and Why It Had to Go

### The Entire Exodus Narrative

**What the full Bible contains:**

The book of Exodus is the story of God commanding Moses to confront Pharaoh — the most powerful ruler on earth — and demand the release of an entire enslaved people. God does not suggest this. God does not recommend patience. God sends plagues. God kills the firstborn of Egypt. God parts the Red Sea and drowns the Egyptian army when they pursue the freed slaves.

The central command repeated throughout: **"Let my people go."**

God's explicit position in Exodus is that enslaving people is a sin severe enough to destroy an empire.

**Why it was removed:**

Because the parallel was exact and undeniable. Enslaved Africans in the Caribbean and American South were the Israelites. The slaveholders were Pharaoh. The plantation was Egypt.

Any enslaved person who heard the Exodus story in full understood immediately that God had commanded the destruction of a slave empire before. The logical conclusion — that God might command it again — was too dangerous to allow.

The missionaries who created the Slave Bible understood this. They did not remove Exodus by accident. They removed it because it was a revolutionary document.

---

### The Book of Isaiah — The Liberation Passages

**What the full Bible contains:**

Isaiah contains some of the most powerful liberation theology in all of scripture.

Isaiah 61:1 — *"The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, because the Lord has anointed me to bring good news to the poor; he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound."*

Isaiah 58:6 — *"Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of wickedness, to undo the straps of the yoke, to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke?"*

Isaiah 10:1-2 — *"Woe to those who decree iniquitous decrees, and the writers who keep writing oppression, to turn aside the needy from justice and to rob the poor of my people of their right."*

**Why it was removed:**

"Liberty to the captives." "Break every yoke." "Woe to those who write oppression."

These passages do not require interpretation. They say directly that God opposes captivity, that God demands the freeing of the oppressed, that God curses those who use law to oppress the poor.

That is a description of the entire slave system. It had to go.

---

### The Book of Amos

**What the full Bible contains:**

Amos is arguably the most politically radical book in the entire Bible. Amos was a shepherd — a poor working man — who delivered God's judgment against the wealthy and powerful for exploiting the poor.

Amos 5:11-12 — *"Therefore because you crush the poor and you exact taxes of grain from him, you have built houses of hewn stone, but you shall not dwell in them... For I know how many are your transgressions and how great are your sins — you who afflict the righteous, who take a bribe, and turn aside the needy in the gate."*

Amos 5:21-24 — *"I hate, I despise your feasts, and I take no delight in your solemn assemblies... But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."*

That last verse is the one Martin Luther King Jr. quoted at the March on Washington in 1963.

**Why it was removed:**

Amos says God hates the religious ceremonies of people who exploit the poor. God does not want your worship. God wants justice. The wealth built on the labor of enslaved people is specifically what Amos condemns.

The slaveholders held church every Sunday. Amos called that an abomination.

---

### Galatians 3:28

**What the full Bible contains:**

*"There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus."*

**Why it was removed:**

This single verse declares that the category of "slave" has no existence before God. In Christ there is no slave. The entire legal and social architecture of slavery — the idea that some human beings are property — is declared void.

Keeping this verse in a Bible handed to enslaved people would have been an admission that God did not recognize their enslavement as legitimate.

---

### The Book of Revelation

**What the full Bible contains:**

Revelation is written in coded language — but it was written by John of Patmos, a political prisoner of Rome, as a condemnation of imperial power. The "Beast" and "Babylon" in Revelation are Rome. The book is fundamentally about God's destruction of an oppressive empire and the liberation of the oppressed.

**Why it was removed:**

An oppressive empire destroyed by God. The powerful brought low. The oppressed vindicated. This is the entire arc of Revelation.

Enslaved people living under the most powerful slave empire in the Western Hemisphere did not need help drawing the parallel.

## What Was Kept — and Why It Was Useful

### Ephesians 6:5-8

*"Slaves, obey your earthly masters with respect and fear, and with sincerity of heart, just as you would obey Christ. Obey them not only to win their favor when their eye is on you, but as slaves of Christ, doing the will of God from your heart. Serve wholeheartedly, as if you were serving the Lord, not people, because you know that the Lord will reward each one for whatever good they do, whether they are slave or free."*

**Why it was kept:**

This passage accomplishes three things simultaneously:
1. Commands obedience to the slaveholder
2. Equates that obedience with obedience to God — making resistance to slavery resistance to God
3. Promises reward in heaven for good service — directing suffering away from action and toward patience

It is the most useful passage in the Bible for a slaveholder. It turns the master's authority into divine authority.

---

### Colossians 3:22-25

*"Slaves, obey your earthly masters in everything; and do it, not only when their eye is on you and to win their favor, but with sincerity of heart and reverence for the Lord. Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters, since you know that you will receive an inheritance from the Lord as a reward. It is the Lord Christ you are serving. Anyone who does wrong will be repaid for their wrongs, and there is no favoritism."*

**Why it was kept:**

Same function as Ephesians 6:5 — obedience to the master is obedience to God. But this passage adds a threat: "Anyone who does wrong will be repaid." Rebellion is not just disobedience to your master. It is sin against God that will be punished.

---

### 1 Peter 2:18-21

*"Slaves, in reverent fear of God submit yourselves to your masters, not only to those who are good and considerate, but also to those who are harsh. For it is commendable if someone bears up under the pain of unjust suffering because they are conscious of God. But how is it to your credit if you receive a beating for doing wrong and endure it? But if you suffer for doing good and you endure it, this is commendable before God. To this you were called, because Christ suffered for you, leaving you an example, that you should follow in his steps."*

**Why it was kept:**

This passage goes further than the others. It explicitly addresses unjust suffering — and commands submission to it anyway. It tells enslaved people that being beaten by a cruel master is commendable before God if endured without resistance. It compares that suffering to Christ's suffering on the cross.

This is the most psychologically sophisticated instrument of control in the Slave Bible. It takes the central symbol of Christian salvation — the suffering of Christ — and uses it to demand that enslaved people accept their own suffering without resistance.

---

### Romans 13:1-2

*"Let everyone be subject to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established. The authorities that exist have been established by God. Consequently, whoever rebels against the authority is rebelling against what God has instituted, and those who do so will bring judgment on themselves."*

**Why it was kept:**

The slave system was the governing authority. This passage says God established it. Rebellion against it is rebellion against God and will bring divine judgment.

The slaveholders did not just own the land and the labor. With this passage they claimed to own God's endorsement.

---

## The Gospel of John — Selectively Kept

**What was kept:**

The parts of John focused on spiritual salvation, eternal life, loving one another within the community of believers, and Jesus as the path to heaven.

**What the full Bible also contains that was inconvenient:**

John 8:32-36 — *"Then you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free... So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed."*

The Slave Bible included some of John but the compilers had to be careful. The language of freedom in John's Gospel was spiritualized — freedom from sin, not freedom from slavery. That distinction had to be maintained carefully.

## What This Document Proves

The existence of the Slave Bible is not a matter of interpretation. It is a physical artifact. A copy is held at Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee. It was displayed at the Museum of the Bible in Washington D.C. in 2018.

What it proves:

**The people who used Christianity to justify slavery knew they were lying.**

They knew the full Bible was dangerous. They knew the Exodus story was a direct challenge to their power. They knew Isaiah's commands to free the oppressed applied to them. They knew Galatians 3:28 said there was no slave before God.

They knew all of this — and they cut it out.

You cannot accidentally remove 90% of the Old Testament. You cannot accidentally keep every passage commanding obedience and remove every passage promising liberation. That is a deliberate editorial decision made by people who understood exactly what they were doing.

The Slave Bible is a confession. It is the slaveholders admitting in the most concrete possible way that the God of the full Bible was not on their side.
