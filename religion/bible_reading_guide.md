# Bible Reading Guide — Oppression vs. Liberty

These are the specific chapters and passages directly relevant to understanding Christianity as both a tool of oppression and a foundation of Black liberation. Read them in order — the sequence matters.

---

## Part One — What They Removed (Read These First)

These passages were cut from the Slave Bible. Reading them tells you exactly what the slaveholders were afraid of.

### Exodus 1-15 — The Liberation Narrative
The foundational text. God commanding the release of an enslaved people. Pharaoh refusing. Ten plagues. The parting of the Red Sea. The Egyptian army drowned.

Read specifically:
- **Exodus 3:7-10** — God hears the cry of the oppressed and commands action
- **Exodus 5:1** — "Let my people go"
- **Exodus 14:13-14** — "The Lord will fight for you"

This is what they cut. Read it and understand why.

---

### Exodus 21:16 — The Slave Trade Condemned
*"Anyone who kidnaps someone is to be put to death, whether the victim has been sold or is still in the kidnapper's possession."*

The transatlantic slave trade was kidnapping. This verse made it punishable by death under Biblical law. They removed it.

---

### Deuteronomy 23:15-16 — Escaped Slaves Protected
*"If a slave has taken refuge with you, do not hand them over to their master. Let them live among you wherever they like and in whatever town they choose. Do not oppress them."*

The Fugitive Slave Act — the law that required escaped slaves to be returned — directly contradicted this passage. They removed it.

---

### Psalms 137 — The Cry of the Captive
*"By the rivers of Babylon we sat and wept when we remembered Zion."*

The entire Psalms was removed from the Slave Bible. This particular Psalm is the lament of a people held in captivity longing for home. The parallel to enslaved Africans torn from their homeland was too direct.

Read **Psalm 137** in full.

---

### Isaiah 58:6 — God Defines True Religion
*"Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of wickedness, to undo the straps of the yoke, to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke?"*

God says directly that freeing the oppressed is what he requires. Not church attendance. Not tithes. Liberation.

Read **Isaiah 58:1-7** in full.

---

### Isaiah 61:1-2 — Liberty to the Captives
*"The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, because the Lord has anointed me to bring good news to the poor; he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound."*

Jesus quotes this exact passage in Luke 4:18 when he declares his mission. The mission of Christ — by Christ's own definition — is liberation of the captive.

They removed Isaiah. They kept the parts of Luke but the connection to Isaiah was severed.

---

### Amos 5:21-24 — God Rejects the Worship of Oppressors
*"I hate, I despise your feasts, and I take no delight in your solemn assemblies... But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."*

God tells the wealthy and powerful that their church attendance means nothing while they oppress the poor. This is the verse Martin Luther King Jr. quoted at the March on Washington.

Read **Amos 5:10-24** in full.

---

### Galatians 3:28 — No Slave Before God
*"There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus."*

The category of "slave" does not exist before God. They removed this verse specifically.

---

### Revelation 18:1-5 — The Fall of Babylon
*"Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great... Come out of her, my people, lest you take part in her sins, lest you share in her plagues."*

Revelation's "Babylon" is a coded reference to Rome — an oppressive empire that will be destroyed by God. The entire book of Revelation was removed from the Slave Bible.

Read **Revelation 18:1-10** — the destruction of the empire.

---

## Part Two — What They Kept (Read These Second)

These passages were deliberately preserved in the Slave Bible. Read them knowing they were selected to control.

### Ephesians 6:5-8 — Obey Your Master
*"Slaves, obey your earthly masters with respect and fear, and with sincerity of heart, just as you would obey Christ."*

Read in full. This was preached to enslaved people every Sunday.

---

### Colossians 3:22-25 — Serve Wholeheartedly
*"Slaves, obey your earthly masters in everything... Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord."*

Read in full.

---

### 1 Peter 2:18-21 — Endure Unjust Suffering
*"Slaves, submit yourselves to your masters with all respect, not only to those who are good and considerate, but also to those who are harsh. For it is commendable if someone bears up under the pain of unjust suffering because they are conscious of God."*

This one goes furthest — it commands submission even to cruel masters and calls that suffering commendable before God.

Read in full.

---

### Romans 13:1-2 — Obey the Government
*"Let everyone be subject to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established... Whoever rebels against the authority is rebelling against what God has instituted."*

The slave system was the governing authority. This passage said God established it.

---

## Part Three — What They Reclaimed (Read These Third)

These are the passages enslaved people found, held onto, and built their liberation theology from — despite everything.

### Luke 4:16-21 — Jesus Declares His Mission
*"The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim freedom for the prisoners and recovery of sight for the blind, to set the oppressed free."*

Jesus defines his own mission as liberation. This stayed in the Slave Bible — but without Isaiah 61 to connect it to, the full weight of the statement was obscured.

---

### Matthew 5:3-10 — The Beatitudes
*"Blessed are the poor in spirit... Blessed are those who mourn... Blessed are the meek."*

The enslaved read this and heard God blessing them specifically. Not the slaveholder. Them.

---

### Acts 17:26 — One Blood
*"From one man he made all the nations, that they should inhabit the whole earth."*

One human family. No inferior race. No cursed people. This verse directly contradicted the racial theology of slavery.

---

### James 5:1-6 — Judgment on the Wealthy
*"Now listen, you rich people, weep and wail because of the misery that is coming on you... Look! The wages you failed to pay the workers who mowed your fields are crying out against you."*

God promises judgment on those who withhold wages from workers. The entire slave economy was built on stolen labor. James says God heard it.

---

### Revelation 21:4 — The Promise
*"He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the old order of things has passed away."*

This is what the hush harbors held onto. Not submission. Not patience. The complete destruction of the old order and the end of all suffering.
