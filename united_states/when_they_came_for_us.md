# When They Came For Us — Black Excellence Destroyed by the System

This is a documented record of black individuals, communities, and movements that built something significant — and were deliberately destroyed by the system. This is not conspiracy theory. Every case here is historically documented.

The pattern is consistent: build, thrive, get destroyed, get erased from history.

---

## Black Wall Street — Greenwood, Tulsa Oklahoma — 1921

**What was built:**
The Greenwood District in Tulsa was the most prosperous black community in American history. Known as Black Wall Street it had:
- Over 300 black owned businesses
- Black owned hotels, hospitals, law offices, and banks
- Black owned newspapers
- A thriving self-sufficient economy where black dollars circulated within the community

**How it was destroyed:**
On May 31–June 1 1921 a white mob — supported by the local government and Tulsa police — attacked Greenwood. The city of Tulsa deputized members of the mob. Private planes were used to shoot at and drop incendiary devices on the community from above.

In 18 hours:
- 35 square blocks burned to the ground
- 1,256 homes destroyed
- 300 black people killed
- 10,000 black people left homeless
- $200 million in property destroyed in today's dollars

**The aftermath:**
No one was ever charged. Insurance claims were denied. The city of Tulsa passed ordinances making it harder to rebuild. The event was deliberately erased from Oklahoma history books for decades. Most Americans still don't know it happened.

## Reconstruction — The South — 1865–1877

**What was built:**
After the Civil War black people used their new political rights to build representation and prosperity across the South:
- Black senators and congressmen elected to office
- Black sheriffs, judges, and local officials
- Black schools and universities founded
- Black economic development and land ownership expanding

**How it was destroyed:**
In 1877 the federal government struck a political deal — the Compromise of 1877 — that ended Reconstruction in exchange for Rutherford B. Hayes winning the presidency. Federal troops were withdrawn from the South.

Without federal protection white supremacist paramilitary groups — the Ku Klux Klan, the White League, the Red Shirts — violently overthrew every black led government in the South. Legally. Systematically.

What followed was:
- Jim Crow laws — legal segregation enforced by the state
- Convict leasing — effectively re-enslaving black people through the criminal justice system
- Systematic disenfranchisement — poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses designed to eliminate the black vote
- Over 4,000 documented lynchings between 1877 and 1950

The system built the shield then removed it the moment it became politically inconvenient.

## Fred Hampton — Chicago — 1969

**What was built:**
Fred Hampton was the 21 year old chairman of the Illinois Black Panther Party. In Chicago he built:
- The Rainbow Coalition — a multiracial alliance between the Black Panthers, the Young Patriots Organization (poor whites), and the Young Lords (Puerto Rican gang turned political organization)
- Free breakfast programs feeding hundreds of children daily
- Free medical clinics
- Negotiated truces between Chicago street gangs turning them toward political organizing

He was building exactly the kind of coalition that could challenge power at a serious level. The FBI knew it.

**How it was destroyed:**
J. Edgar Hoover's FBI COINTELPRO program classified Hampton as a major threat. On December 4 1969 Chicago police officers working with the FBI conducted a predawn raid on Hampton's apartment.

Fred Hampton was shot twice in the head at point blank range while asleep in his bed. He was 21 years old.

His pregnant fiancée was sleeping next to him.

The initial police report claimed Hampton fired first. Forensic evidence later proved the police fired nearly 100 rounds. Evidence showed Hampton had been drugged by an FBI informant before the raid so he could not wake up or resist.

No officers were ever charged with a crime.

**The aftermath:**
The Rainbow Coalition collapsed without Hampton's leadership. The FBI's goal was achieved. The most dangerous organizer in Chicago was eliminated before he could fully build what he was building.

## Marcus Garvey and the UNIA — 1920s

**What was built:**
The Universal Negro Improvement Association was the largest black mass movement in history — Garvey claimed 6 million members across the United States, Caribbean, and Africa, though historians estimate 1–2 million at peak. Garvey built:
- The Black Star Line shipping company — black owned, black operated
- Negro Factories Corporation — promoting black owned businesses
- A global Pan-African movement connecting the diaspora
- A newspaper — The Negro World — with international distribution

**How it was destroyed:**
J. Edgar Hoover — the same FBI director who later destroyed Fred Hampton — personally targeted Garvey from the beginning. Hoover called Garvey "the most dangerous black man in America."

The FBI infiltrated the UNIA with informants, surveilled Garvey's every move, and worked to build a legal case against him. Garvey was convicted of mail fraud in 1923 in a case many historians consider politically motivated. He was imprisoned then deported to Jamaica in 1927.

Without Garvey the UNIA collapsed. The largest black mass movement in history was dismantled through legal persecution of its leader.

## The Black Panther Party — National — 1966–1982

**What was built:**
Founded by Huey P. Newton and Bobby Seale in Oakland California the Black Panthers built:
- Free breakfast programs that fed 20,000 children daily nationally — later adopted by the US government as the federal school breakfast program
- Free health clinics
- Community schools
- Legal aid programs
- Armed patrols monitoring police brutality in black communities

**How it was destroyed:**
COINTELPRO — the FBI's counterintelligence program — specifically targeted the Black Panthers as the number one threat to American internal security. Methods used:

- Forged letters sent between Panther chapters to create internal conflict
- Assassination of leaders — Fred Hampton being the most documented
- Fabricated criminal charges to imprison members
- Planting informants and agent provocateurs to incite violence
- Leaking disinformation to newspapers to destroy public image

By the late 1970s the organization had been effectively destroyed through a combination of imprisonment, assassination, and internal conflict deliberately engineered by the FBI.

## Martin Luther King Jr. — 1968

**What was built:**
By 1968 King had evolved far beyond the sanitized version taught in schools. He was:
- Publicly opposing the Vietnam War — alienating the Johnson administration
- Organizing the Poor People's Campaign — a multiracial economic justice movement targeting wealth inequality
- Planning a massive occupation of Washington DC demanding economic rights

He was building a movement that threatened economic power not just social segregation. That was a different and more dangerous thing.

**How it was destroyed:**
The FBI under Hoover surveilled King for years, wiretapped his phones, tried to blackmail him, and sent him an anonymous letter urging him to commit suicide.

On April 4 1968 King was assassinated in Memphis Tennessee. James Earl Ray was convicted of the murder. In 1999 a civil lawsuit — King family vs Loyd Jowers — resulted in a jury finding that King's assassination was the result of a conspiracy involving government agencies. That verdict received almost no mainstream media coverage.

## The Pattern

Look at every case above and notice what is consistent:

1. Black people build something that works — economically, politically, or organizationally
2. The system identifies it as a threat
3. The system deploys legal persecution, infiltration, violence, or all three
4. The leadership is imprisoned, deported, or killed
5. The movement collapses without its leadership
6. The event is erased or minimized in mainstream historical record

This is not coincidence. This is a documented pattern of deliberate suppression.

Understanding this pattern is essential for understanding [[Malcolm X]] — he lived inside this pattern his entire life. His father Earl Little was a Garveyite whose house was burned down and who was later killed under suspicious circumstances. Malcolm himself was surveilled by the FBI, infiltrated by informants, and assassinated at 39 years old.

The pattern didn't happen to him. He was born into it.
