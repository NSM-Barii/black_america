# COINTELPRO — The FBI's War on Black America

---

## What It Was

**COINTELPRO** — Counter Intelligence Program — was a series of covert and often illegal programs run by the FBI under J. Edgar Hoover from **1956 to 1971**.

Its stated purpose was to surveil and disrupt domestic political organizations deemed threats to national security. In practice its primary targets were **Black political organizations and leaders** — from the moderate NAACP to the Nation of Islam to the Black Panther Party.

The program was not discovered through journalism or whistleblowers. It was exposed by activists who broke into an FBI field office in **Media, Pennsylvania on March 8, 1971** and stole thousands of documents. Those documents were leaked to the press and later confirmed by the **Church Committee** — a Senate investigation in 1975–1976 that documented the program's full scope.

The FBI did not deny what the documents showed. They were real.

---

## The Targets

COINTELPRO ran operations against a wide range of organizations but its Black-focused operations were the most extensive:

- **The NAACP** — surveilled from the 1940s onward
- **The Nation of Islam** — infiltrated with informants; Malcolm X was surveilled from the early 1950s
- **Martin Luther King Jr. and the SCLC** — Hoover personally despised King and ran the most intensive surveillance operation against him
- **Marcus Garvey** — Hoover began targeting Garvey in 1919, before COINTELPRO formally existed, calling him "the most dangerous black man in America"
- **The Black Panther Party** — classified by Hoover as "the greatest threat to the internal security of the country"
- **SNCC** — the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee
- **The Revolutionary Action Movement**
- Individual leaders including **Stokely Carmichael**, **H. Rap Brown**, **Elijah Muhammad**

---

## The Methods — Documented

COINTELPRO used methods that were illegal under the laws of the time and would be prosecuted as crimes if done by private citizens:

**Infiltration**
FBI informants were planted inside every major Black organization. These informants:
- Reported on internal plans, membership, finances, and communications
- Actively worked to create distrust and conflict within organizations
- In some cases helped plan and execute operations against their own organizations

**Forged Letters and Documents**
The FBI forged letters, memos, and other documents designed to create conflict between organizations and individuals. Documented examples:
- Forged letters sent between Black Panther chapters across the country to create the impression that leaders in different cities were conspiring against each other — deliberately inciting internal violence
- Forged letters sent to gang leaders falsely claiming the Panthers had put out contracts on them
- Anonymous letters sent to Elijah Muhammad making false accusations about Malcolm X — contributing to the atmosphere that led to Malcolm's assassination

**Anonymous Threats**
The FBI sent anonymous threatening letters to activists and their families. To King they sent a letter in 1964 that included recordings of his extramarital affairs and an implicit instruction to commit suicide:

> *"King, there is only one thing left for you to do. You know what it is."*

This was sent by the FBI of the United States government to a sitting civil rights leader.

**Fabricated Criminal Charges**
Members of targeted organizations were arrested on fabricated or exaggerated charges. The goal was imprisonment, financial ruin from legal costs, and destruction of the organization's capacity to function.

**Leaking Disinformation to the Press**
The FBI leaked false and misleading information about targeted individuals and organizations to newspapers and television stations. Much of it was printed without verification.

**Tax Investigations**
The IRS was used to audit and financially pressure targeted individuals and organizations.

**Assassination**
The most extreme method — and the most documented in the case of Fred Hampton. See [[foundations/when_they_came_for_us_black_excellence_destroyed_by_the_system]].

## Martin Luther King Jr. — The Full Picture

Hoover's obsession with King was personal as well as political. He called King "the most dangerous Negro in America."

The FBI's campaign against King included:
- Wiretapping his home, office, and hotel rooms across the country
- Recording his extramarital affairs and using the recordings as blackmail material
- Sending recordings to King's wife Coretta
- The 1964 suicide letter
- Attempting to replace King as the movement's leader with someone more "acceptable" — they tried to elevate **Samuel Pierce** and others
- Monitoring every meeting, speech, and conversation for years

When King won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964 Hoover called him "the most notorious liar in the country" at a press conference.

After King's assassination in 1968 the FBI's surveillance files showed they had detailed knowledge of threats against his life in the period leading up to the murder. Whether they had advance knowledge of the specific assassination plot is contested. That they surveilled him obsessively and did nothing to protect him is documented fact.

---

## Malcolm X — The Full Picture

The FBI began surveilling Malcolm X in 1953 — the year after he was released from prison and joined the NOI.

By the early 1960s his FBI file ran to tens of thousands of pages. Informants were embedded at every level of his organizations.

After he left the Nation of Islam in 1964 the threat environment intensified. The FBI documented:
- NOI death threats against Malcolm
- The firebombing of his home in February 1965
- The general atmosphere of danger in the weeks before his assassination

The FBI had informants present at the Audubon Ballroom on February 21, 1965. The NYPD had undercover officers present. Neither intervened.

The 2021 vacating of two of the three convictions in his assassination — after evidence emerged that the FBI and NYPD had withheld exculpatory information — is the most recent legal development in a case that has never been fully resolved.

---

## The Church Committee — 1975

Senator Frank Church led a Senate Select Committee investigation into intelligence agency abuses. The committee's final report documented COINTELPRO's operations in detail and concluded:

> *"The Committee finds that the domestic activities of the intelligence community at times violated specific statutory prohibitions and infringed the constitutional rights of American citizens."*

No FBI official was ever prosecuted for COINTELPRO operations. J. Edgar Hoover died in 1972 — before the program was exposed — and avoided all accountability.

The program was officially discontinued in 1971. The methods it used — infiltration, informants, surveillance, disruption of political organizations — did not end. They were institutionalized.

---

## The Legacy

COINTELPRO directly destroyed:
- The Black Panther Party — through assassination, imprisonment, and deliberate internal conflict
- Marcus Garvey's UNIA — through legal persecution and deportation
- Malcolm X's organizational capacity — through infiltration and the atmosphere that led to his assassination
- Martin Luther King Jr.'s final campaigns — through surveillance, blackmail, and the assassination itself

The pattern is consistent and documented. Every time Black Americans built an organization that seriously threatened the existing power structure the federal government deployed its resources to destroy it.

Understanding this is essential for understanding why Black political organizing in America is structurally more difficult than it appears from the outside. It is not a failure of Black political will. It is the documented result of sustained government suppression.
