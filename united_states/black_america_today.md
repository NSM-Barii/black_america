# Black America Today — The Current State

> *"The statistics are not random. Every number in this note has a historical cause you can trace through this vault."*

---

## How to Read This Note

These numbers mean nothing in isolation. Every gap documented here is the direct result of specific historical policies — slavery, convict leasing, Jim Crow, redlining, the GI Bill exclusion, COINTELPRO, the War on Drugs, mass incarceration. Read those notes first. Then come back here.

A gap is not a mystery. It is a result.

---

## Wealth

| | Black | White | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median family wealth | ~$24,000 | ~$188,000 | 1:8 |

**The wealth gap is the most important number.** Income can change in one generation. Wealth — property, investments, inheritance — accumulates across generations.

Black families were legally prohibited from building wealth for:
- **246 years** of unpaid labor under chattel slavery
- **100 years** of Jim Crow, redlining, and exclusion from GI Bill benefits
- Ongoing asset destruction — Black Wall Street (1921), white mob violence against Black property owners throughout the 20th century

The current wealth gap is not the result of present-day choices. It is the accumulated result of 400 years of deliberate policy.

## Unemployment

| | Black | White |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rate | ~5.5–6% | ~3.5% |

Black unemployment has been approximately **2x white unemployment** for every decade on record — through recessions, booms, Democratic administrations, and Republican administrations. This consistency is not explained by individual economic cycles. It reflects structural barriers in hiring.

## Homeownership

| | Black | White |
|---|---|---|
| Homeownership rate | ~44% | ~73% |

The homeownership gap is **larger today than when the Fair Housing Act was passed in 1968.**

The Fair Housing Act made discrimination in housing sales illegal. It did not undo:
- The wealth gap that prevents down payments
- Credit score systems that disadvantage people with interrupted employment histories
- Neighborhood property value disparities created by decades of redlining
- Ongoing documented discrimination by real estate agents, mortgage lenders, and appraisers

## Education

| | Black | White |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's degree or higher | ~28% | ~40% |

**Counter-trend:** Black women are the fastest-growing demographic in higher education in the country. Black women now attend college at higher rates than Black men and are outpacing most other demographic groups in degree attainment growth.

**School funding inequality** remains a structural driver of educational gaps. American public schools are primarily funded by local property taxes. Black communities with lower property values receive less school funding. This is legal, documented, and ongoing.

## Incarceration

| | Black | White |
|---|---|---|
| Share of prison population | ~38% | ~39% |
| Share of general population | ~13% | ~60% |
| Lifetime incarceration risk (men) | 1 in 3 | 1 in 17 |

Black men are incarcerated at **5x the rate** of white men.

**5.2 million Americans** are disenfranchised due to felony convictions. 1 in 13 Black Americans of voting age cannot vote as a result. This directly reduces Black political power.

## Health

| | Black | White |
|---|---|---|
| Life expectancy | ~71 years | ~76 years |
| Maternal mortality rate | 2.6x higher | baseline |
| Infant mortality rate | 2x higher | baseline |

**Black maternal mortality** is one of the starkest health disparities in the developed world. Black women in America die from pregnancy-related causes at 2.6x the rate of white women — including Black women with college degrees and high incomes. Research increasingly points to the cumulative physiological effects of chronic stress from racial discrimination — called **weathering** — as a significant factor independent of socioeconomic status.

## The Reparations Debate

Given this documented history of wealth extraction and systematic exclusion, the question of reparations — financial compensation to Black Americans for the ongoing effects of slavery and Jim Crow — has re-entered mainstream political debate.

**The argument for:**
- The wealth gap is directly traceable to specific government policies — unpaid labor, land theft, GI Bill exclusion, redlining
- Germany paid reparations to Holocaust survivors. The US paid reparations to Japanese Americans interned during World War II. The precedent exists.
- The gap will not close naturally — economic modeling suggests it would take over 200 years at current rates without intervention
- H.R. 40 — a bill to study reparations — has been introduced in every Congress since 1989 without passing

**The argument against (stated):**
- People alive today didn't own slaves
- It would be logistically impossible to implement
- It would be divisive

**The honest assessment:**
The "people alive today didn't own slaves" argument ignores that people alive today benefit from wealth built on slave labor and lose economic position relative to white Americans due to specific government policies of the 20th century — redlining, GI Bill exclusion — that are within living memory, not ancient history.

The debate is ongoing. No federal reparations program exists as of 2026.

## The DEI Rollback — 2025–2026

A significant political development currently underway: the rollback of **Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion** programs across corporate America and federal government.

In 2025 the Trump administration issued executive orders eliminating DEI programs across the federal government and pressuring federal contractors to do the same. Major corporations including Goldman Sachs, Ford, Harley-Davidson, and others eliminated or scaled back their DEI programs under political and legal pressure.

**The context:** DEI programs were themselves a limited response to the documented disparities above — not reparations, not structural change, but incremental corporate and institutional efforts to increase representation. Their elimination, without any alternative mechanism for addressing documented disparities, means the gaps documented in this note will widen.

## The Bottom Line

Every single gap in this note has a traceable historical cause:

| Gap | Primary Historical Cause |
|---|---|
| Wealth gap | 246 years unpaid labor + century of asset exclusion |
| Homeownership gap | Redlining, GI Bill exclusion, ongoing discrimination |
| Income gap | Employment discrimination, educational inequality |
| Incarceration gap | War on Drugs, mandatory minimums, racially disparate enforcement |
| Health gap | Chronic stress, healthcare access inequality, environmental racism |
| Political representation gap | Disenfranchisement, gerrymandering, historical exclusion |

None of this is mysterious. All of it is documented. The only question is whether the society has the will to address the causes rather than managing the symptoms.
