# Reconstruction — 1865 to 1877

> *"The slave went free; stood a brief moment in the sun; then moved back again toward slavery."* — W.E.B. Du Bois

---

## What Reconstruction Was

Reconstruction was the period immediately following the Civil War when the federal government attempted to reintegrate the former Confederate states and define what freedom would actually mean for 4 million formerly enslaved people.

It lasted 12 years. It was the closest America ever came to actually delivering on its founding promises to Black people. And it was deliberately and violently destroyed.

---

## The Constitutional Promises

Three amendments were passed during Reconstruction:

**13th Amendment (1865)** — abolished slavery. With one critical exception: *"except as punishment for crime."* That exception was immediately exploited. See [[slavery_by_another_name]].

**14th Amendment (1868)** — granted citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the United States and guaranteed equal protection under the law. This was the first time Black people were constitutionally recognized as citizens.

**15th Amendment (1870)** — granted Black men the right to vote. Women of all races were excluded.

On paper these were transformative. In practice the federal government's willingness to enforce them determined everything.

---

## What Black People Built During Reconstruction

When given legal protection and political rights for the first time, Black Americans moved immediately and decisively.

**Political representation:**
- **Hiram Revels** — first Black US Senator, Mississippi, 1870
- **Blanche Bruce** — second Black US Senator, Mississippi, 1875
- **Joseph Rainey** — first Black member of the House of Representatives, South Carolina, 1870
- Over **2,000 Black men** held political office across the South during Reconstruction — senators, congressmen, governors, sheriffs, judges, school board members

**Institutions built:**
- The **Freedmen's Bureau** — established by Congress to assist formerly enslaved people with food, housing, employment, legal matters, and education
- **Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs)** — Howard University (1867), Morehouse College (1867), Fisk University (1866), Hampton University (1868) — all founded during or immediately after Reconstruction
- Black churches as community centers, political organizing hubs, and schools
- Black newspapers
- Black land ownership expanding rapidly — some estimates suggest Black Southerners owned 15 million acres of land by the end of Reconstruction

**The "40 Acres" Promise:**
General William Sherman's Special Field Order No. 15 in January 1865 designated 400,000 acres of confiscated Confederate land along the South Carolina and Georgia coast for distribution to formerly enslaved families in 40-acre plots. This is where "40 acres and a mule" comes from.

President Andrew Johnson reversed the order in the fall of 1865 and returned the land to its Confederate owners. The promise was made and broken within the same year.

## The Violence Against Reconstruction

From the moment Reconstruction began white supremacist organizations formed specifically to destroy it through terror.

**The Ku Klux Klan** — founded in Pulaski, Tennessee in 1865 by former Confederate general Nathan Bedford Forrest. Its explicit purpose was to restore white supremacy through murder, arson, and intimidation of Black voters and officeholders.

**The White League** — Louisiana, 1874. Paramilitary organization that openly massacred Black militiamen and Republican officeholders. In the **Battle of Liberty Place** in New Orleans (1874) the White League killed 11 Metropolitan Police officers and 3 others and seized control of the city government for several days.

**The Red Shirts** — South Carolina and Mississippi. Operated similarly — armed intimidation of Black voters at polling places, assassination of Black political leaders.

The federal government responded with the **Enforcement Acts of 1870–1871** and temporarily suppressed the Klan. But the political will to sustain federal enforcement was eroding.

---

## The Compromise of 1877 — The Betrayal

The 1876 presidential election between Republican Rutherford B. Hayes and Democrat Samuel Tilden was disputed — the electoral votes of three Southern states were contested.

A political deal was struck — the **Compromise of 1877**:

- Hayes would be awarded the presidency
- In exchange federal troops would be withdrawn from the South
- Southern Democrats would be allowed to "redeem" their states — meaning restore white Democratic control

Federal troops left. Without federal protection every Black led government in the South was overthrown. The paramilitary organizations that had been temporarily suppressed resumed operations openly.

Reconstruction was over.

---

## The Aftermath — What Replaced It

Within years of the federal withdrawal:

- Every Black elected official in the South was removed from office through violence, fraud, or intimidation
- **Black Codes** were replaced by **Jim Crow laws** — legal segregation enforced by the state
- Convict leasing rebuilt forced labor under a new legal framework
- Poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses, and white primaries systematically eliminated the Black vote
- Lynching became the primary tool of racial terror — over 4,000 documented between 1877 and 1950

The land Black families had accumulated was seized, stolen, or lost under legal and extralegal pressure. By the mid-20th century Black land ownership in the South had been largely reversed.

---

## Why This Matters

Reconstruction proves that Black political and economic advancement was possible when legally protected. It also proves that the system would destroy that advancement the moment it became politically inconvenient to protect it.

This is the **Reconstruction Precedent** referenced in [[the_central_question_build_within_or_outside_the_system]] — the historical proof that using political power as a shield for Black advancement works until the shield is removed.

Every Black political and economic setback from 1877 onward has roots in what was destroyed during the end of Reconstruction.
